**Settings is the one panel that is not the island** — an ordinary window,
moved and closed like any other, where an option that is a shape is drawn as
the thing it changes.

## Opening it

- <kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>,</kbd>
- The right button on the wallpaper → **Settings**
- The **Settings** door on the [control centre](/shell/control-centre/)'s top row
- `>` in the [launcher](/shell/launcher/), which lists the shell's own places

Opening it closes whatever the island had open. **Search settings**, at the top
of the sidebar, finds a page by its name, its parts or what is on it, in
English or in Spanish, and the arrows at the top go back and forward through
the pages you visited.

## The pages

Eleven pages in three groups — **The shell**, **The desk** and **The session**.
A long page is split into parts, drawn as a strip under its title.

| Page | Parts | What it holds |
|---|---|---|
| [Bar & Island](/shell/bar/) | The island, The bar, Workspaces, Notifications | The bar's style and the island at rest, the time and what runs beside it, what the bar carries and how each piece reads, the workspaces, notifications and Do not disturb |
| [Desktop](/shell/desktop/) | Module settings, The widgets | The weather's location, whose GitHub contributions, how a note is written; the way into arranging, and the look every widget follows |
| [Control Centre](/shell/control-centre/) | — | The way into arranging the control centre, and the doors on its top row |
| [Dock](/shell/dock/) | — | The edge, the alignment, the size, how it behaves, and the applications kept on it |
| [Launcher](/shell/launcher/) | Results, Sigils, Clipboard | How results are ordered and how many, the first character for each mode, the clipboard history |
| Appearance | Theme, Windows, Type, Motion | The theme and wallpaper, the transition, the greeting scene; window rounding, gaps, opacity, blur, glass and shadow; the interface and monospace fonts; the pace of the animations |
| [Displays](/shell/displays/) | Arrangement, The screen, The lid, Night light | The screens, the laptop lid and the night light |
| Input | Keyboard, Pointer | Keyboard layouts and key repeat; sensitivity, the cursor's colour and size, shake to find |
| [Keys](/shell/keys-and-packages/#changing-a-key) | The shell's own, The compositor's | Every key, for the profile in use |
| [Session](/shell/lock-and-login/) | Lock screen, When you leave | Your name and picture, the lock's blur, the idle waits |
| System | Profiles, This machine | Profiles and Reset; the language, and the machine with the version installed |

The wallpaper and the palette themselves are chosen in the island: Appearance →
Theme opens them there ([Palettes](/theming/palettes/)).

- **A setting made meaningless by another stays on the page, dimmed**, and says
  which setting is responsible. What the machine lacks is dimmed the same way:
  the glass and shake to find say to run `./setup plugins`, the night light
  that hyprsunset is not installed.
- **A slider's number is a field**: click it, type, <kbd>Enter</kbd>.
- **Every group is one card**, its rows ruled off by hairlines. The ⓘ beside a
  group's name opens its note, which says what the group does and what to know
  before changing it; a click, not a hover, so the page holds still.
- **The help button at the top right**, a question mark in a circle, opens
  this site at the page you are on, or at the part of it.
- **Everything that reaches Hyprland is kept by the shell** and applied again
  after every reload. The Lua files in `~/.config/hypr/` are the fallback, and
  the window never writes them.

## Profiles

**A profile keeps a whole desk under a name**, and the one in use takes every
change as you make it: there is no save button. System → Profiles lists them,
the one in use outlined, each with its wallpaper and a line for its bar, its
dock and how many widgets it has.

| In a profile | Kept by the machine, whatever the profile | In no profile |
|---|---|---|
| The bar, the widgets, the dock, the launcher, the control centre, the look, **every key**, the wallpaper and its palette | The screens and the lid, your name and picture, the language, the keyboard layouts, the weather's location, the GitHub user, Do not disturb, the night light | Notes, tasks, the clipboard, the pets, the game bests |

### The three that ship

| Profile | Bar | Widgets | Wallpaper |
|---|---|---|---|
| Moon castle | One island | Analogue | `japanese-castle-full-moon.jpeg` |
| Fuji | Grouped bar | Modern | `pagoda-fuji-blossom.jpeg` |
| Night bay | Spread bar | Modern | `tropical-bay-night.jpeg` |

A fresh install starts on **Moon castle**. Each of the three carries every key,
the same set in all three, and each is laid out for a 1920 × 1200 screen. Each
is added to the list once: one you delete stays deleted, and one an update
brings is added.

### Working with them

- **Switch**, on a card, makes it the profile in use. Switching reloads
  Hyprland, so anything set by hand with `hyprctl` goes with it. Profiles are
  switched here and nowhere else.
- **New profile** adds one and opens its name for typing; it is not switched
  to.
- The **⋯** menu on a card: **Rename**, **Duplicate**, **Export to a file…**,
  and **Delete** — not on the one in use, and confirmed on the card, because
  there is no undo.
- **Import…** adds a profile from a file, without switching to it. A file that
  does not parse, or is not a profile, is refused whole.
- **Reset this profile**, under the list, puts every setting in the profile in
  use back to its default and reloads Hyprland. The machine's settings are
  kept, and there is no undo.

An export is plain JSON: the name, the wallpaper's path, the palette and the
settings. It carries the path and not the picture, so on a machine without that
painting the wallpaper already there stays up.

The profile in use is `~/.local/state/quickshell/settings.json`, and the others
wait in `profiles.json` beside it. An export is the backup.

## Language

**System → This machine → Language: English or Español.** It is the language of
the settings window: it never changes the system's language or renames a
folder in your home, and anything without a translation stays in English.
