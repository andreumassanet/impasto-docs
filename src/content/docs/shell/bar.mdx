**Three styles, one layout.** Pieces are dragged out of a catalogue onto a
picture of the bar, then drawn grouped round the island, spread to the edges,
or all inside one capsule. Changing the style keeps what is on the bar.

<Figure
  name="bar-styles"

## Styles

Chosen under Settings → Bar & Island → The island → **Style**.

| Style | How it is drawn |
|---|---|
| Grouped | The workspaces, the island and the modules together in the middle. The default. A detail opening pushes the sides out. |
| Spread | The workspaces at one edge, the modules at the other, the island between them. The sides hold still. |
| One island | Everything inside a single capsule. Whatever the island becomes, the whole band changes shape into it. |

In One island, **Span the whole screen** runs the band the full width of the
bar instead of fitting what is on it. **Island** sets whether the island floats
below the top edge or is cut into it as a notch, in every style.

**Ground** is **Classic**, the island's black, or **Glass**, the terminal's own
ground: the accent over a blur of what is behind, with a lit edge. The desktop's
widgets and the dock follow it unless they are given a ground of their own.

## Sides

**The sides can sit straight on the wallpaper.** Under Settings → Bar & Island
→ The island → **Sides**, *In capsules* draws each group on its black capsule;
*On the wallpaper* drops the capsules and leaves the icons and figures, white
with a tight shadow so they read on a light picture as well as a dark one. The
island keeps its capsule either way. In the One island style the sides already
sit on its band, so the setting is locked there.

## What goes on it

- **Modules** — each a piece that shows a reading and opens a detail in the
  island when clicked. The same click closes it.
- **The workspace strip** — a mark for each of the desk's workspaces, the one
  this screen shows lit; a click brings that workspace to this screen. It is
  drawn in one of seven styles ([below](#size-and-workspaces)).
- **The tray** — the icons applications put in the system tray, in one
  capsule (below).
- **Buttons** — a symbol and no figure: every door of
  [the control centre](/shell/control-centre/#the-top-row), the control
  centre itself and Capture. They are Launcher, Workspace overview, Control
  centre, Capture, Appearance, Notes, Task board, Games, Keys, Packages,
  Settings and Session menu. Each opens its panel and stays lit while it is
  open; Settings opens the window, and Capture does what the capture key does
  — the photo at once, with the surface's last choices. Statistics and the pet
  are modules here, not buttons.
- **Split** — ends one capsule and starts the next.

Neighbouring modules share a capsule; the workspace strip and the tray are
always a capsule of their own. A capsule holding one piece takes that piece's shape: a ring is a
circle, a button a circle, a symbol and its figure a pill.

Out of the box the left side holds the workspaces, and the right holds
Notifications, Network, Bluetooth, Volume and Battery.

A piece the machine cannot show — no battery, no screen that can be dimmed, no
Bluetooth adapter, no grim for Capture — is not drawn, even when placed.
Nothing on the bar moves by itself: a piece is where you put it.

## Arranging

<Steps>

1. Open Settings (<kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>,</kbd>) → Bar & Island → **The bar**.
   Under *Layout* is a picture of the bar in its style, with the
   real time in the island, and the catalogue under it.

2. Drag a piece from the catalogue onto the picture. Let go over the left half
   and it goes left of the island; over the right half, right of it.

3. Drag a piece along the bar to move it, or off the bar to take it off.

4. Click a piece to pick it. A card under the bar has **Remove**, and the look
   of that piece alone.

</Steps>

The catalogue is four groups: the modules that measure, the modules that read
out a state or a count, the buttons, then the workspace strip and the split.
It always holds every piece, and taking from it copies, so a piece can be on
the bar twice. There is no on or off switch anywhere: on the bar is
on.

## The tray

**The applications' tray icons, in one capsule, drawn grey** so they keep to
the bar's black. Place the Tray piece from the catalogue like any other; it is
not drawn while no application has an icon in it. Past five icons, the rest
fold behind a count, and a click on the count shows them.

| On an icon | Does |
|---|---|
| Click | The application's own action, usually opening its window |
| Right click | Opens the application's menu in the island |
| Middle click | Its second action, where it has one |
| Wheel | Scrolls it, for the applications that listen |

The menu is drawn by the shell, so every application's menu looks like the
island. An entry with a submenu replaces the list with it, and the chevron in
the header steps back out. <kbd>Up</kbd> and <kbd>Down</kbd> move,
<kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Right</kbd> opens, <kbd>Left</kbd> goes back.

## How a piece reads

Two settings for the whole bar, under The bar → *Chips*:

| Setting | Choices |
|---|---|
| Shape | **Icon** — the module's symbol, small (the default). **Ring** — its gauge as a circle round the symbol: charge, volume, a countdown. |
| Figure | **No** — the symbol alone. **On hover** — the figure opens under the pointer. **Always** — always beside it (the default). |

Only a module whose reading fills from empty to full gets a ring. Network,
Bluetooth, Notifications, Weather, Updates and Calendar keep their symbol in
either shape.

<Figure name="bar-modules" alt="The same bar four times: the symbols alone, the symbols with their figures, the rings, and the rings with their figures" caption="Icon or ring, with its figure or without, for the whole bar." />

**Any module can have a look of its own.** Pick it on the picture, and its card
offers Shape and Figure, each with *Like the bar* to follow the whole bar.
Media and Timer also have **When**: *Always*, or *While it runs*, to be on the
bar only while something is playing or counting.

Every module has a figure, so Always means all of them. Where there is nothing
to count it is a word: *Off*, *No devices*, *Muted*, *0:00*, *Egg*,
*Nothing playing*.

Warnings do not follow the palette. Off the charger, the battery turns yellow
at 20% and red at 10%, and the System piece steps to the same hues at 70% and
90% processor load.

## The module catalogue

| Module | On the bar | Its detail in the island | Needs |
|---|---|---|---|
| Media | The track's title, or *Nothing playing* | The player: artwork, title, artist, progress, previous, play or pause, next | A media player on the session bus (MPRIS) |
| Timer | A ring that drains, and the time left | Idle, set a countdown; running, hold, restart or stop it | — |
| Claude | Claude's mark, and the session's percentage — or its tokens, until the percentage is known | Tokens and messages for the five-hour session and the week, the account's own percentages, and when the session resets | Claude Code (below) |
| Codex | Codex's mark, and the share used of the fullest limit still running | Each limit the plan sets, its share used and when it resets, and when it was read | Codex (below) |
| Battery | The charge | The charge, charging or the time left, the power going in or out, and the energy stored | A battery |
| Volume | The level, or *Muted* | A slider, and mute buttons for the output and the microphone | — |
| Brightness | The level of the screen you are on | A slider for each screen that can be dimmed | A backlight, or a monitor that answers DDC/CI |
| Network | The network's name, or *Off* | The Wi-Fi list, the same as the control centre's | — |
| Bluetooth | The connected device, a count, *No devices* or *Off* | The device list | A Bluetooth adapter |
| Notifications | A bell and how many are kept; crossed out in do not disturb | The history, with Clear | — |
| Weather | The conditions and the temperature | The place, feels-like, the reading's age and the next hours | A network connection (below) |
| System | The processor load | Processor and memory, with their recent history | — |
| Updates | How many updates are waiting | What is waiting, when it was checked, and Open for the packages panel | pacman-contrib for a fresh count |
| Pet | The pet that is out, and its level or *Egg* | Feed, play, and the shelf of the whole family | — |
| Calendar | Its symbol and the day | The month, with a dot under each day that has tasks; such a day opens its list | — |

- **Claude** appears once Claude Code has written its transcripts under
  `~/.claude/projects`; the token and message counts are read from them. The
  percentages are the account's own, the ones `/usage` shows: while the piece
  is on screen, every ten minutes the shell sends the smallest possible
  request to the API, signed in with Claude Code's own credentials, and reads
  the answer's headers. The token is never stored or printed.
- **Codex** appears once Codex has written a session log under
  `~/.codex/sessions` (or `$CODEX_HOME`). The limits are read from the end of
  its newest logs, which Codex rewrites on every turn, so nothing is sent
  anywhere and the figures are as old as the last turn. A limit past its reset
  shows no share until Codex runs again.
- **Weather** comes from wttr.in, which guesses the place from your connection
  when none is set. Set it under Settings → Desktop → Module settings →
  **Location**: a city, a postcode or an airport code.
- **Updates** counts the repositories and the AUR, and never installs: Open
  goes to [the packages panel](/shell/keys-and-packages/), which runs the
  upgrade in a terminal. Without pacman-contrib the count is from the last
  sync.
- **System** — the full panel, with cores, disks and temperatures, is
  <kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>U</kbd>.

**Not modules on the bar:** Notes, Tasks, Games and GitHub are desktop widgets,
and notes, the task board and the games are buttons that open their panels —
see [the desktop](/shell/desktop/), [notes and tasks](/shell/notes-and-tasks/)
and [games and pets](/shell/games-and-pets/). GitHub needs a username under
Settings → Desktop → Module settings → GitHub → **Username**. The clock is the
island itself.

## Zen

<kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>CTRL</kbd> <kbd>B</kbd> puts the bar away and gives its
band to the windows. The island still comes down, on its own, for a
notification, the volume or brightness, or a panel you open by its key, and
goes back up when it is done; the desktop's widgets spread over the whole
screen meanwhile. Press it again for the bar. The same switch is **Zen** under
Settings → Bar & Island, and `qs ipc call bar toggle` flips it from anything
else.

## Size and workspaces

Under Settings → Bar & Island → The island → *Scale*: **Bar height** (24–48 px),
**Top margin** (0–32 px) and **Side margin** (0–48 px). Everything on the bar
scales with its height; as a notch, the island ignores the top margin.

Under Settings → Bar & Island → **Workspaces**: *Workspaces shown* are always
drawn on the strip (five by default); the rest, up to *Workspaces available*
(ten by default), appear only while they have windows.

**The strip draws in one of seven styles**: Dots, the default, Bars or Rings,
or the workspace's number written out — Numbers, Roman, Kanji or Greek. The
written styles mark the one in view with an accent pill that slides from one
to the next. It is one setting for the whole bar, chosen from tiles on the
Workspaces tab or on the strip's own card in the bar's layout.

<Figure name="workspace-styles" alt="The workspace strip seven times: dots, bars, rings, then the numbers written as digits, roman numerals, kanji and greek letters, each with the workspace in view lit in the accent" caption="Dots, bars, rings, numbers, roman, kanji and greek." />

For a bar without the strip, the workspace can sit beside the time on
[the island](/shell/island/) instead.

Every screen draws the same bar, and the one with the keyboard is the live
