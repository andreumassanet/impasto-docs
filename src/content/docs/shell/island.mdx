**The island is one object that changes shape.** A black capsule in the middle
of the bar rests on the time and becomes everything else: a glance under the
pointer, the control centre, the launcher, the overview, a notification, a
game. Each shape is the size of what is in it, with no title and no close
button.

## At rest

**The island rests on the time.** Under the pointer it lights, and a click
opens the [control centre](/shell/control-centre/).

**What is running sits either side of the time**: a recording, what is using
the microphone, the camera or the screen, a countdown, a track, a virtual
machine and the workspace, in that order, two at most. A recording is always
shown, and first; the rest are switched under Settings → Bar & Island →
**Beside the time** (see [Settings](#settings)). One running thing splits
across both sides, its mark on the left and its figure on the right; two take
a side each. What is running past those two is counted at the island's end as
a small **+N**, and every one of them is in [the glance](#the-glance).

- A recording is a red dot and the minutes. A click on the dot stops it; under
  the pointer it squares off to say so.
- Privacy is a glyph for each of the microphone, the camera and the shared
  screen in use, in fixed orange, green and blue, and the program's name on the
  other side of the time. It opens nothing, so the glance and a click work over
  it as they do over the time. On by default.
- A countdown is a ring that drains and the time left. A click opens its
  detail.
- A track is its artwork and a spectrum that follows the sound. A click opens
  the player.
- A virtual machine is its system's mark and how long it has run, or how many
  run when there are several. A click opens [the machines](/shell/machines/).
- The workspace is a mark and the number, or the name, of the workspace the
  screen shows, for a bar without the workspace strip. It is always there when
  switched on, so it comes last. A click opens [the overview](#the-overview).
  Off by default.

## The glance

**Rest the pointer on the time and a glance opens at once**; it closes a moment
after the pointer leaves. It is always one face: a square at the near end,
what is in it beside the square with its controls, and at the far end the time
large with the day and the date under it.

- **The face is the running thing with the most to press**: the music first,
  then a countdown, a virtual machine, a recording, and what is using the
  microphone, the camera or the screen.
- **Everything else running is a chip** on a strip under the face: its mark,
  its name and a reading.
- **The square is never an empty box**: a cover fills it, and anything else is
  drawn large in it: the countdown's ring, the recording's dot, the machine's
  or the weather's mark.
- **With nothing running**, the face is the weather, when the weather is
  already on the bar or on the desktop, and otherwise the time alone, centred.

| Face | Its controls |
|---|---|
| Music | Previous, play or pause, next; under the title, the line being sung |
| Countdown | Restart, pause or resume, cancel |
| Virtual machine | Open its window, pause or resume, shut down |
| Recording | Stop |

<Figure name="glance" alt="The island opened into a glance: the cover of Resurrections by Lena Raine, a painting of Mount Fuji, with previous, pause and next under the track, and at the far end 19:32 large over MONDAY 28 SEPTEMBER." caption="The glance with a player: the cover, the track, its controls and the time." />

**The square and a chip do what that thing's mark does at rest**: the cover
opens [the lyrics](#lyrics), a countdown its detail, a machine the machines
panel, and a recording's dot stops it. The square, the chips and the controls
are the only things on it to press; a click anywhere else opens the control
centre. It does not open while a notification or a reading holds the island,
or over a running thing's mark. Turn it off with **A glance on hover** (see
[Settings](#settings)).

## Lyrics

**The lyrics of whatever is playing come from lrclib.net**, timed per line,
with any player. The glance carries the line being sung under the track's
title, and the [desktop](/shell/desktop/#lyrics) has a widget that shows the
words and nothing else.

**Click the music, on the bar, in the glance or at rest, and once its lyrics
are known it opens out round them**: the cover large on one side with the
track, the progress and the controls under it, and the lyrics on the other,
the line being sung lit and held in the middle. A click on a line goes to that
place in the song. Lyrics that are not timed are the whole text, to scroll,
with nothing lit; a track lrclib does not know opens the plain player.

Every lookup tells lrclib.net what is playing, so one switch turns all of it
off: Settings → Bar & Island → **Lyrics**. It is on by default, and nothing is
looked up while nothing on screen shows lyrics.

## Opening and closing

**What opened a shape closes it**: the same key, the same click, Escape, or a
click anywhere off the island. Opening another shape replaces it. While a
panel or a detail is open it holds the keyboard, and the pointer can cross the
windows without taking it back.

<Figure
  name="island-details"
  alt="Module details in the island: the player, the battery, Claude Code's usage, the processor and memory, the volume, the Wi-Fi list, the weather, the pet, the month with task dots, the tasks, a countdown and the GitHub wall"
  caption="Every module on the bar opens into the island."
/>

**Every piece on the bar opens into the island** rather than into a popup of
its own, one at a time. A click on the same piece closes it; a click on
another swaps it. What each one shows is in
[the module catalogue](/shell/bar/#the-module-catalogue).

**When several things want the island at once**, a panel you opened comes
first, then a notification, then a passing reading, then the glance. A reading
that arrives while a panel is open is dropped rather than shown late, and
opening a panel sends a notification on screen away — it stays in the list.

## Notifications

**The shell is the notification daemon**, and a notification arrives in the
island as one row: the sender's picture with the application's mark on its
corner, or the application's icon, or a bell; the title, with the application
and the time at its far end; and two lines of the body.

<Figure name="notification" alt="The island opened into a notification from Vesktop: Laura's picture with the Vesktop mark, Are we still on for the market at nine?, and two buttons at the end, Reply in the accent and Later." caption="A notification with two buttons, in the island." />

- **Buttons** the application sends sit at the end of the row, up to two, the
  first in the accent. More than two, or longer ones, share the width in a row
  underneath.
- **A click** runs the notification's default action, or closes it when it has
  none. **A right click** always closes it. There is no close button.
- A notification with no timeout of its own stays as long as **Bar & Island →
  Notifications → How long one stays** says: two to fifteen seconds, five by
  default. One that asks for longer is held to fifteen. A critical one stays
  until you close it, and a newer ordinary one does not replace it.
- Every notification is kept, newest first, up to fifty: in the bell's detail
  on the bar and in the control centre's notification block. A click on a row
  runs its default action while the notification is still open. Under the
  pointer a row shows a close mark that removes it, and **Clear** empties the
  list.
- **The list is kept across restarts** and logins, in the shell's state
  directory. A notification that asks not to be kept is shown and not stored.
  Inline replies are not shown.

**Do not disturb** keeps notifications off the island and still keeps them in
the list; a critical one comes through anyway. The bell is crossed out while
it is on, and it stays on across a restart. Switch it with the **Focus** tile
in the control centre, or in Settings → Bar & Island → Notifications.

<Aside>
If another notification daemon is running — mako, dunst, swaync — it holds the
name first, and the island shows nothing. Stop it, and the shell takes over by
itself.
</Aside>

## Passing readings

**What you change shows as one passing capsule**: the volume, the brightness,
and the charger plugged in or out, each as a symbol, a level bar and the
reading. Holding a key gives one smooth reading rather than a flicker of
them, and it falls away after under two seconds. With the volume or brightness
detail open, its own slider shows the change instead.

<Figure name="osd" alt="The island as a narrow bar with a speaker symbol, a volume slider and 72%." caption="The volume, passing." />

The same capsule carries messages with no level: what a capture did, the
colour picked, a recording starting and stopping, a new best in a game, a new
egg for the pet, a profile switched, the window layout cycled, game mode on or
off.

## Asking for root

**The shell is the password dialog.** When a program asks for root through
polkit, `pkexec` or any other, the island opens on the request: *Authentication required* and what the program wants,
the lock screen's pill for the password, and under it the action asked for,
with **Cancel** and **Authorize**.

- A refused password shakes the pill and says *That password didn't work*.
- While Caps Lock is on, the line under the pill says so.
- Closing the island any way, <kbd>Escape</kbd> included, answers no.
- It comes over a full-screen window as well.

Should the shell fail to take the role, the KDE password dialog does it
instead until the next login.

## The overview

**<kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>TAB</kbd> shows every workspace at once**, five to a
row, up to the number set under Settings → Bar & Island → Workspaces →
Workspaces available. Each cell is a scale model of the screen its workspace is
on, scaled to fit: every window is drawn live, at its own place and size. The
workspace you are on has a thin outline, and the one Enter goes to a thick one.

| Do | And |
|---|---|
| Arrow keys, or move the pointer | move the outline; it wraps at the edges |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd>, or click a cell | bring that workspace to the screen the overview is on |
| <kbd>Escape</kbd> | close the overview |
| Click a window | focus it |
| Right-click a window | close it |
| Middle-click a window | float or tile it |
| Drag a window onto another cell | move it to that workspace; you stay where you are |
| Drag a window within its own cell | a floating window is put where it lands; a tiled one swaps with the tiled window under it |

## Settings

Under Settings → Bar & Island, in Shape, Clock and Beside the time:

| Setting | What it does |
|---|---|
| Style | Grouped, Spread or One island — see [the bar](/shell/bar/#styles) |
| Island | Floating below the top edge, or Notch, cut into it |
| Span the whole screen | One island only: the band runs the width of the bar |
| On every screen | *One on each, and the one you are on is the live one*, or *Only on the screen you are on*. Dimmed with one screen |
| A glance on hover | Off, only a click opens anything |
| Lyrics | Off, no lyrics anywhere, and nothing is looked up; see [Lyrics](#lyrics) |
| Clock format | 24-hour or 12-hour |
| Show the date, Show seconds | What the time carries |
| Beside the time | Privacy, Timer, Media, Virtual machines and Workspace, each on the island or not; see [At rest](#at-rest) |

## On a second screen

**Every screen draws the whole bar, and one of them is live**: the screen with
the keyboard. The live bar opens panels and shows notifications; the others
show the island at rest. The island moves only while it is shut, so an open
panel holds it where it is. See [Several screens](/shell/screens/).
