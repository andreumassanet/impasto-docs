**Any module can live on the wallpaper.** Widgets sit on a grid under the
windows, and each one is a module the bar and the island already know — the
same player, the same battery, the same weather. The desktop and the bar do not
consult each other, so a module can be on one, on both or on neither, and the
same module can be on the grid twice: a clock as a band and another as a dial.

## The grid

**Everything sits on a grid, and nothing overlaps.** A widget's place is a
column and a row, and the gap between two widgets is the gap between two
windows. The grid starts under the bar and stops short of the dock when the
dock reserves its space; a floating dock draws over the bottom row.

A widget dropped on an occupied square goes to the nearest free one, or back
where it came from if the board has no room. The widgets already there never
move aside.

| Shape | Cells |
|---|---|
| Square | 2 × 2 |
| Card | 4 × 2 |
| Large square | 4 × 4 |
| Band | 8 × 2 |

The shapes are not one design at four sizes: each says a different amount, so
a module offers only the shapes it has something to put in. Every module has a
card.

## Arranging

**Right-click the wallpaper and choose Arrange widgets.** The desktop lifts
above the windows, the grid is drawn, and a tray of every module slides up
along the bottom, each tile a live miniature of its square.

<Figure name="arrange" alt="The desk in arranging mode: widgets on a faint grid, the weather widget selected with a card beside it for its shape, face, style and opacity, and a tray of every module along the bottom." caption="Arranging: the tray along the bottom, and one widget's card open." />

- **Add** — drag a tile from the tray onto the grid; the squares it will land
  on are lit before you let go. A click on a tile puts it on the first free
  square instead.
- **Move** — drag the widget.
- **Change its shape** — pull the handle in its bottom-right corner. The shape
  changes as the pointer crosses from one footprint to the next, never
  stretching in between. The mouse wheel over a widget steps through its
  shapes too.
- **Remove** — the ⊖ badge in its top-left corner, or drop the widget on the
  tray.
- **Give it a look of its own** — click it (below).

The badge and the handle appear under the pointer and on the selected widget.
**Done** on the tray, or the right button anywhere, ends arranging. The same
mode opens from Settings → Desktop → The widgets → Edit, which closes the
settings window meanwhile. Arranging never takes the keyboard.

### The right button

At rest, the right button opens a menu where it was pressed:

| On | The menu |
|---|---|
| The wallpaper | Arrange widgets, New note, Wallpaper, Palette, Settings |
| A widget | Edit — arranging, opened on that widget's card — and Remove |
| A note | Open, Edit and Remove |

Wallpaper and Palette open the island's pickers ([Palettes](/theming/palettes/)).
The left button on the wallpaper does nothing but close a menu, and the menu
has no keyboard: a click elsewhere closes it.

## Each widget's own look

**Click a widget while arranging and a card opens beside it** — to its right,
its left or under it, wherever there is room.

| On the card | |
|---|---|
| Shape | The shapes the module offers, drawn as footprints — the same choice as the corner handle |
| Face | Modern or Analogue |
| Style | Capsule, Accent, Outline or No capsule |
| The slider | How solid the capsule is, 20 to 100 %, with a button that puts it back |
| Remove | Takes the widget off |

<Figure name="desktop-styles" alt="The same five widgets in four styles over a coastal wallpaper: Capsule, Accent, Outline and no capsule" caption="Capsule, Accent, Outline and no capsule, on the same five widgets." />

The first tile under Face and under Style is **Default**, marked with a dot: it
follows the desktop's own setting, and a widget keeps following it until you
pick something else. The line under the styles says what the widget is wearing.
The card closes on a click on the wallpaper, and when a drag starts.

Settings → Desktop → The widgets holds what every widget follows by default:
**Face**, **Style** and **Background**, the opacity.

| Style | What it draws |
|---|---|
| Capsule | The island's black ground with a hairline edge |
| Accent | The colours turned inside out, with the accent as the ground |
| Outline | The edge alone, over the wallpaper |
| No capsule | The contents straight on the wallpaper |

Outline and No capsule shadow the type so it reads on the picture. With the
background lowered, the wallpaper shows through blurred.

**The ground is the island's black and everything else is the palette**, so
every widget repaints with the wallpaper. Warnings keep their fixed colours — a
battery in the red is red in every palette — and the GitHub wall keeps GitHub's
grey to green. There is no palette per widget.

## Two themes

**Modern is a figure with a caption; Analogue draws each module as an object**,
read by where something is rather than by a number. Either one is a choice per
widget.

Modern lays every widget out on the same grid — the mark top left, the name top
right, the reading along the bottom — so a wall of them reads as a set, and a
bigger shape is the same widget saying more: a card fills in the right, a large
square the middle. The player's sleeve, the month and the GitHub wall are drawn
whole rather than as a reading. Analogue keeps the figure small under the object
on a square and puts the words beside it on a card.

| Module | Shapes | Analogue draws |
|---|---|---|
| Clock | square, card, band | a dial, which is also offered as a large square |
| Calendar | square, card, large | a leaf off a wall calendar |
| Weather | all four | the sky, with a thermometer beside it |
| Media | square, card, large | a record that turns while it plays |
| System | square, card, large | a gauge with a needle |
| Claude | square, card, large | a fuel gauge from E to F, without the large square |
| Battery | square, card | a cell that fills |
| Timer | square, card | an hourglass |
| Volume | square, card | a knob |
| Brightness | square, card | a fader |
| Network | square, card | arcs |
| Bluetooth | square, card | the Bluetooth rune |
| Updates | square, card | a parcel with the count on its label |
| Tasks | square, card, large | a clipboard |
| Pet | square, card | the pet on a line of ground |
| Games | square, card | a joystick |
| GitHub | square, card, band | the wall of contribution squares, in both themes |
| Notes | all four | paper, in both themes |

A widget switched to a theme with no face for its shape shrinks to the largest
shape that fits inside the old one, so nothing grows into a neighbour.

**Every widget is always drawn.** With nothing to report it says so — "Nothing
playing", "No battery", "No forecast" — and keeps its square; the only way for
a widget to leave is Remove. The weather's location, whose contributions the
GitHub wall draws and how a note is written are in Settings → Desktop → Module
settings, and the bar's chips read the same settings.

On the calendar, a day with a dot has tasks due. Press it and the widget turns
into that day's tasks; it comes back when the pointer leaves it, or from the
arrow. See [Notes and tasks](/shell/notes-and-tasks/).

## Notes on the edges

**A deck is notes stacked along the left, right or bottom edge**, as tabs that
peek out. At rest each note is a sliver of its colour; the pointer arriving
brings the tabs out with their titles, and the tab under it slides the whole
note out beside it. A click opens the note in the island, and the right button
offers Open and Edit.

<Gallery columns={2} items={[
  { name: 'deck-rest', alt: "The left edge of the wallpaper with three thin coloured slivers, notes tucked against the edge.", caption: "A deck at rest." },
  { name: 'deck-peek', alt: "The same edge with the tabs pulled out and one note, Sunday, shown beside its tab.", caption: "The tab under the pointer slides its note out." },
]} />

While arranging:

- drag the Notes tile from the tray against an edge, and the newest note starts
  a deck there;
- drag a tab along its edge to reorder it, to another edge to move it, onto the
  grid to make it a square of its own, or onto the tray to take it off;
- drag a note widget against an edge and it joins the deck there;
- drag the round grip before the first tab to slide the whole deck along;
- click a tab for the deck's card: **Where** (left, right or bottom), **Along
  the edge** (Start, Middle or End) and **Which notes**, ticked on or off.

There is one deck per edge at most, and none on the top, which is the bar's. A
note is in one place at a time: putting it on an edge takes it off its square.
A notes widget on a square shows one note — the newest, or the one picked under
**Which note** on its card — and a click opens it in the island.

Decks hide under a full-screen window. Settings → Desktop → Module settings →
**Edges only on an empty workspace** hides them whenever a window is open on
the workspace, and brings them back when the last one closes.

## Screens and sizes

**The widgets and the decks are on one screen**: the one that carries the
island, chosen in Settings → Displays → Arrangement → The island is on (see
[Displays](/shell/displays/)). A widget is filed by its square and nothing
else, so a second screen would draw the same widgets twice. Other screens get
the dock and the bar without the island.

A square is the same size on every screen, so a smaller screen has fewer of
them. A layout made on a bigger screen is pulled in to fit when it is drawn,
and the saved places are left as they were, so it comes back whole on the
bigger screen. The three profiles that ship are laid out for 1920 × 1200.

What the desktop does not do is listed under [Limitations](/help/limitations/).
