## The lock screen

**<kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>L</kbd> locks the session, and the shell draws the lock
itself** through the compositor's session lock, so a shell that crashes leaves
the machine held rather than open.

**At rest it is the clock alone**: the date over the time, the hours over
softer minutes. The island sits at the top, where the bar has it, holding a
padlock, and the battery ring is in the top right corner. The desk stays behind
it, blurred enough that text on it cannot be read.

<Figure name="lock-rest" alt="The lock at rest over the blurred desk: Monday, 21 September over a large 22 and softer 00, the island at the top holding a padlock, and the battery ring in the top right corner." caption="At rest: the clock alone." />

**Any key wakes it, and that key only wakes it**: it is not typed into the
password, so a space pressed to open the screen is not a first character. A
lone Shift or Caps Lock does not wake it; a click does, and so does opening
the lid.

- Awake, the clock rises and steps back, and one black pill comes up with your
  picture, your name and "Enter your password" — "Face unlock or password"
  where [face unlock](#face-unlock) is set up.
- From the first character, the pill stretches into the field.
- Restart and shut down are in the bottom-left corner while it is awake, each
  armed by the first click and run by the second.

<Figure name="lock-awake" alt="The lock awake: the clock risen towards the top, a black pill at the bottom with a round picture, the name andreu and Face unlock or password, and restart and shut down in the bottom-left corner." caption="Awake: the pill, and the power buttons in their corner." />

The password goes through PAM's `login` configuration, so hyprlock is not
needed. A wrong one shakes the pill and says why under it. <kbd>Escape</kbd>
clears the field and puts the screen back to its clock, and so do 30 seconds
untouched, never while a password is being checked. Unlocking fades the type
and relaxes the blur, so the desk is what is left when the lock falls; with two
screens the blur stays.

Settings → Session → Lock screen holds **You**: a name and a picture for the
lock screen — click the picture, or drop an image on the card. Left empty,
both come from your account, as on the login screen. Under **Clock**,
**Style** is Stacked or Inline, hours over minutes or both on one line; the
login screen always draws it stacked. Under **Background**, the **Blur** is 8
to 64 px, previewed over the wallpaper.

### When you leave it alone

**The shell is its own idle daemon.** Settings → Session → When you leave has
three waits:

| Slider | What happens |
|---|---|
| Lock after | The session locks |
| Screen off after | The screen switches off |
| Suspend after | The session locks, then the machine suspends |

Each is in minutes, from 0 to 60, and 0 reads **Never**. All three are off
until you set them. A video player, or a browser playing full screen, asks the
compositor not to count as idle, and nothing happens while it plays. Suspend
always locks first, and is skipped if the lock does not come up.

**Every sleep locks first**, not only these: shutting the lid, a suspend key,
`systemctl suspend`. The shell asks the system to wait before sleeping, locks,
and lets it sleep once the lock is up, so the machine never wakes on the desk.
With a monitor plugged in, shutting the lid does not sleep and so does not
lock.

The screen comes back with the next key or movement, and
<kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>SHIFT</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd> switches it off and on by hand.

<Aside type="tip">
Set the screen to go off after the lock, not before. The lock takes its picture
of the desk as it goes up, and a screen that is already dark gives it a plain
ground instead. The slider says "before the lock" when it is set that way.
</Aside>

## Face unlock

**With an infrared camera, the lock opens for your face.** `./setup face`
finds the camera by the 8-bit grey picture only an infrared one gives,
installs howdy-next from the AUR, points it at that camera and asks to enrol
your face. `./setup install` runs it once and skips a machine without one.

howdy looks through a PAM service of the lock's own,
`/etc/pam.d/impasto-face`, beside `login` rather than inside it: a face it does
not know never costs a password try, and the password never waits on the
camera. `./setup system` installs that file, and install does it unless
`--skip-system`.

- **A scan starts when the lock wakes** — a key, a click or the lid opening —
  and again, while it is awake, on the pointer moving or <kbd>Enter</kbd> on
  an empty field. Never at rest and never by itself, so a nudged mouse does
  not turn the camera on, and the face that has just pressed
  <kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>L</kbd> does not open it again.
- **While it looks, the island grows** under the camera and a ring of ticks
  turns round the padlock. A match closes the ring in green and opens the
  padlock; a miss turns it red and shakes the island.
- After a miss it waits a second and a half. After three, only
  <kbd>Enter</kbd> on an empty field asks again.
- The password works all along, at rest or awake.
- Going back to rest stops a scan under way.

<Figure name="lock-scan" alt="The lock looking for a face: the island at the top grown into a black square round the padlock, with a ring of ticks turning about it." caption="Looking: the island grows under the camera." />

Only the lock uses it: the login screen, sudo and polkit still ask for the
password. `sudo howdy add` enrols another face, and `sudo howdy config` opens
howdy's own settings — how sure a match must be, how long it looks, which
camera.

## The login screen

**The login screen is the lock with the desk taken away**: an SDDM theme with
the same island and padlock, the same stacked clock and the same two stages,
over a painting of brush strokes blurred and darkened as a whole. It does not
follow the palette, because it comes before there is a user to have chosen one.

- At rest it is the clock alone. A key wakes it and is kept as the first
  character, and a click wakes it too; <kbd>Escape</kbd> or 30 seconds
  untouched puts it back.
- **One pill holds the account and the field.** With more than one account,
  the picture is the account picker: pressing it opens the list above the
  pill. <kbd>Escape</kbd> closes an open list before it rests the screen.
- The padlock opens when the password is accepted. A refused one shakes the
  pill and empties the field.
- The session is chosen in the bottom-right corner, and restart and shut down
  are in the bottom-left, both only while it is awake. The power buttons are
  armed by the first click and run by the second. There is no suspend.
- The battery ring sits in the top right, read straight from the battery.
- The line under the pill says when Caps Lock is on.

The account and session lists take the pointer only; the keyboard stays in the
field.

### How it starts

**`./setup install` switches it on**, unless `--skip-system`: it copies the
theme into `/usr/share/sddm/themes/impasto/`, names it in
`/etc/sddm.conf.d/10-impasto.conf`, and enables sddm where no display manager
is enabled yet. The login screen comes up at the next boot.

A machine that already starts another display manager keeps it. To change,
disable that one, then run `sudo systemctl enable sddm`.

SDDM reads the files in `/etc/sddm.conf.d/` in name order and `/etc/sddm.conf`
last, each over the one before, so a theme named in a file that sorts after
`10-impasto.conf` wins. Leave `10-impasto.conf` as it is: `setup` owns it.

To try the theme without logging out, run its test mode from the repository's
root. It reads the repository directly, so a change can be tried before it is
installed:

```bash
sddm-greeter-qt6 --test-mode --theme system/usr/share/sddm/themes/impasto
```

<Aside>
The power buttons are not drawn in test mode, because SDDM reports them
unavailable there. A theme that fails to load says why in red on SDDM's own
fallback screen, not in the terminal.
</Aside>

### Your picture

**The picture on both screens is `/var/lib/impasto/faces/<username>.face.icon`**,
yours to put there; without one, both show your initials. `setup` makes the
folder and never removes it. It is kept outside your home because SDDM reads
as its own user and cannot see into it.

```bash
sudo install -m 644 ~/picture.png /var/lib/impasto/faces/$USER.face.icon
```

The lock screen looks there first, then at `~/.face`, `~/.face.icon` and
AccountsService. The name beside the picture is your account's full name, which
`chfn` sets, or the login name when there is none. The lock screen can take
another name and picture from Settings → Session; the login screen always reads
the system's.

What the two screens do not do is listed under
[Limitations](/help/limitations/).
