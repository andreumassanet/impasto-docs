**<kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>Y</kbd> opens the time spent in each application**, by
the day, the week or the month. The shell counts while the session is in use
and writes it down on the machine; nothing leaves it.

## The panel

**Day, Week or Month, behind a switch at the top**, and arrows to step back
through earlier ones.

- **The total, and how it compares**: a day against the week before it, a week
  or a month by its daily average against the one before.
- **Bars** per hour of the day, or per day of the week or month, stacked in
  shades of the accent by what the time went to.
- **When**: the day's sessions as a ribbon, or the week as a grid of weekdays
  by hours.
- **Most used**: the applications, or their groups (Development, Social,
  Games and the like, read from each application's own categories), with a
  switch between the two.
- **First use**, **Unlocks**, **Notifications** and the **Longest session**.

The first day there is little to show, and the panel says screen time counts
from now on.

## On the bar and the desk

**The Screen time module puts today's total on a chip.** Its detail is the day
in brief: a ring by group, the hours, the three most used, and **Open** for the
whole panel. On [the desktop](/shell/desktop/#three-themes) it comes in all
four shapes in Modern, as a 24-hour dial in Analogue, a session per arc with
midnight at the top, and as a sticker in Sticker, with a ring that fills as the
day reaches the week's average.

**Screen time** is also a door for the
[control centre's top row](/shell/control-centre/#the-top-row), and one of the
shell's places after `>` in [the launcher](/shell/launcher/).

## What counts

**The time counts while the session is unlocked**, for the window in front. It
stops when the screen locks, or when the idle setting turns the screen off, and
starts again when you are back. A screen switched off by hand, with
<kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>SHIFT</kbd> <kbd>D</kbd>, still counts.

**Only the window's class is kept, never its title**: which application, not
what was in it. Each day is one file in `~/.local/state/quickshell/screentime/`,
written at least every five minutes, so a crash loses no more than that. Days
older than about thirteen months are removed.

<Aside>
A suspend nobody locked for ends the open span where the clock stopped, so a
night with the lid shut does not count as a night at the screen.
</Aside>
