## The arcade

**<kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>G</kbd> opens a shelf of eleven games, and the island
becomes the board.** The arrows slide the shelf, <kbd>Home</kbd> and
<kbd>End</kbd> go to either end, and <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> plays
the game in the middle; a click on a side tile brings it to the middle. The
shelf reopens on the game played last, and the line under it is that game's
best, or "Not played yet".

While a game is on, the strip over the board carries the score — or the moves —
and the best, and nothing else. The score beats when it changes.

| Key | |
|---|---|
| <kbd>Escape</kbd> | Back to the shelf; from the shelf, closes the island |
| <kbd>R</kbd> | Starts the round again, at any moment |
| <kbd>Enter</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> | Plays again once the round is over |

A finished round washes the board back and ends on a card that lands over it:
"Game over" — "Solved" for the puzzles — the score, or "★ New best" when it
beat the best, and **Play again**; a click on the wash does the same. A beaten
best throws a ring of sparks behind the card. Closing the island leaves the
game, and the round with it.

The arcade is also on the wallpaper, as the Games widget, and in the
[control centre](/shell/control-centre/), as a block: both show the latest
bests and open the shelf.

| Game | What it is | Controls |
|---|---|---|
| Whack-a-Mole | Nine holes laid out like a phone keypad, in 45-second rounds; a miss costs two seconds | <kbd>1</kbd>…<kbd>9</kbd>, or click |
| Snake Sprint | Ten points an apple, and a little faster with each one | Arrows |
| Flood Colors | Flood the board from the top-left corner in as few moves as you can | <kbd>1</kbd>…<kbd>6</kbd>, or click a colour |
| 2048 Mini | 2048 on a 4 × 4 board, until nothing can merge | Arrows |
| Lights Out | A press flips a light and its four neighbours; turn all 25 off in as few presses as you can | Arrows and <kbd>Space</kbd>, or click |
| Hextris | Slabs fall onto the sides of a hexagon; three of a colour touching clear, and a stack nine deep ends the round | <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> rotate, <kbd>↓</kbd> or <kbd>Space</kbd> drops; click the outer thirds to rotate, the middle to drop |
| Bot Bash | Robots march down five lanes; three reaching the floor end the round, and a bash on an empty lane costs a life | <kbd>1</kbd>…<kbd>5</kbd>, or click a lane |
| Target Smash | One shrinking target at a time — three for the bullseye, two for the middle ring, one for the outer — and three lives | The pointer only |
| Space Blaster | A formation of twenty invaders that speeds up as it thins; three lives | <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> move, <kbd>Space</kbd> fires |
| Tetris | A 10 × 20 well, falling faster every ten lines | <kbd>←</kbd> <kbd>→</kbd> move, <kbd>↑</kbd> rotates, <kbd>↓</kbd> soft drop, <kbd>Space</kbd> hard drop |
| Solitaire | Klondike, dealt one card at a time; click a card to pick it up and click where it goes | The pointer; <kbd>Space</kbd> turns the stock |

The games follow the palette like everything else: each one's colours are the
wallpaper's. The boards are drawn with volume — the moles and the robots have
bodies and faces, Tetris's blocks are bevelled, Solitaire's cards cast on the
ones beneath — and a hit is paid with a burst where it lands. The snake slides
between its cells rather than jumping.

### Bests

**One best per game**, kept in `~/.local/state/quickshell/games.json`: the
highest score, or the fewest moves for Flood Colors, Lights Out and Solitaire.
A round that scored nothing is not recorded. A beaten best says so over the
board and, when it was not the first, on the island. There is one number per
game and nothing else — no history, no names.

## The pets

**A family of five small creatures lives on the bar**: one out at a time, the
rest asleep on a shelf. Each one hatches from an egg, earns levels from being
fed and played with, and never dies. The five are Dot, Sprout, Ember, Sol and
Drift, each in one of the palette's colours, and each can be given a name of
its own.

### Looking after one

<kbd>SUPER</kbd> <kbd>SHIFT</kbd> <kbd>P</kbd> opens the pet's panel: the one
that is out, its mood, how far it is to the next level, and **Feed** and
**Play**. Its name is edited in place. The Pet chip on the bar has the same
Feed and Play in its detail.

<Figure name="pet" alt="The pet panel: Pip, the blue pet that is out, with its mood, its level bar, Fed and Play, and the rest of the family listed below with their levels and Bring out." caption="Feed, Play, and the family on its shelf." />

| | Experience |
|---|---|
| Feed | 25, once every 90 minutes |
| Play | 12, once every five minutes, plus three for every hour since the last game, up to 18 more |
| Being out | 1 every five minutes, while the pet is on the bar and awake and the screen is unlocked |

An egg hatches at its first meal or its first game, and never on its own, and
the creature is whole from the moment it hatches. Each level takes a little
more than the one before, and a star arrives at level 15.

The mood is read off when it was last fed and played with: **Beaming**,
**Content**, **Peckish** — a snack is owed — **Lonely** — nobody has played
today — or **Fast asleep**. None of them costs anything.

### The family

Under the pet that is out is the family, each with its level and **Bring out**.
**Only the pet that is out earns anything**; the others sleep on the shelf, and
the time asleep does not count against them.

Another egg is laid when the family's levels added together reach 6, then 16,
30 and 50, and it hatches into one of the species the family does not have yet
— so the five are found by taking turns rather than by playing harder with one.
With all five found, a star waits at level fifteen for each.

**Nothing dies, and a week away costs nothing.** A neglected pet is hungry,
lonely or asleep, and nothing is ever reset. The family is kept in
`~/.local/state/quickshell/pet.json` and is the same in every profile. The
games earn it nothing.

### Four styles

**The same five are drawn in one of four styles**, chosen at Settings →
Desktop → Module settings → Pet → **Style**. The species decides the colour and
what the creature is; the style decides how it is drawn, and every style wears
the mood on its face. The drawing is the same on the bar, in the pet's panel
and on the desktop.

| Style | |
|---|---|
| Creature | A different animal for each species. The default. |
| Plush | One round body, shaded. |
| Paper | Flat, cut from two tones. |
| Pixel | A sprite, sixteen cells across. |
